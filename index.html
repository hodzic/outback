About

Plan a paddle, hike or bike ride online, then take it offline. Your trips, tracks and settings stay on this phone.

Tides and currents: NOAA CO-OPS predictions. Wind, weather, pressure and elevation: Open-Meteo. Lightning: NWS thunder probability (US), else estimated. Maps: NOAA electronic charts (NOAA Chart Display Service), OpenStreetMap, USGS topo and imagery, OpenSeaMap sea marks, OpenStreetMap restrooms, parking and boat ramps; launch sites from the San Francisco Bay Area Water Trail, and your own overlay PDF maps. Auto-align of overlay PDF maps: water outlines from OpenStreetMap (Overpass API). Ships: AIS from aisstream.io. Time estimates use your set speed; hiking adds 1 hour per 2000 ft of climb, biking per 3000 ft. Tracks come from GPX files you import, e.g. from your watch.

Not for navigation or safety decisions. Forecasts, tides, currents, maps, elevation, ship positions and time estimates come from free public services and simple calculations. They can be wrong, late, incomplete or missing, and saved data goes out of date offline. Small boats, many hazards, and trail or road closures don't show at all.

Before any paddle, hike or ride: check official forecasts, warnings and local conditions; carry proper charts or maps, a compass and the safety gear your trip needs; tell someone your plan and when you'll be back; and turn back when in doubt. You are responsible for your own safety.

Help

Install on your phone

Installing puts Outback on your home screen. It opens full screen and keeps its offline data.

Android (Chrome)

  1. Open this page in Chrome.
  2. Tap the ⋮ menu, then Install app (or Add to Home screen).
  3. Tap Install.

iPhone and iPad (Safari)

  1. Open this page in Safari.
  2. Tap the Share button (square with an arrow), then Add to Home Screen.
  3. Tap Add.

From then on, open Outback from its home screen icon.

Using it offline

Outback works offline once each trip has been prepared while you still have a connection.

Before you leave

  1. Open the trip, choose the map layer you'll use (Chart, Street, Topo or Sat for satellite), then tap Get forecast. Wait until the status says the map was saved offline.
  2. Open the Map view once and zoom in to the detail you'll want.
  3. Add the app to your home screen. iPhones can clear saved data for websites that aren't installed and haven't been used for a while.

Works offline

  • The app itself, once you've opened it online.
  • Your trips: routes, settings, recorded tracks and overlay PDF maps are stored on the phone.
  • The forecast you already fetched: tides, currents, wind, pressure, lightning and elevation. It can't refresh; the status line says when it was fetched.
  • GPS: your location on the map (◎) uses satellites, not data.
  • Map tiles that Get forecast saved around the route, and areas you've already looked at.
  • SF Bay Water Trail launch sites (🛶), always. Restrooms (🚻), parking (P) and other launches in areas you've looked at with them on, and around the route if they were on during Get forecast.

Needs a connection

  • Getting or refreshing a forecast, or changing tide stations.
  • Tides and currents on the map (≈) for stations and days you haven't looked at yet. Ones you've seen are kept.
  • Map areas you haven't saved or viewed, and map layers you didn't save (Get forecast saves only the layer selected at the time).
  • Importing your first overlay PDF map (the PDF reader downloads on first use). Overlay PDF maps you've imported show fine offline.
  • The first Auto-align of an overlay PDF map (it gets OpenStreetMap water for the area once, then keeps it with the map).
  • AIS ships: they are live only and need a data connection.
AIS ships

On paddle trips, the AIS button at the bottom right of the map shows large vessels nearby, from their AIS radio broadcasts collected by aisstream.io.

Get a free key (once)

  1. Go to aisstream.io and tap Sign In (with a GitHub account).
  2. Open API Keys and create a new key. Copy it.
  3. In Outback, tap AIS on the map and paste it, or paste it in Settings above.

The key is stored only on this phone. Type demo instead to see made-up ships.

On the map

  • Colour shows the type: cargo, tanker, passenger/ferry, tug, pilot or patrol, high-speed, grey for other or not yet known. Bigger icons are longer ships; faded ones are stopped.
  • The dashed line is where the ship will be in the next 15 minutes at its current speed and course, with a dot every 5 minutes.
  • Tap a ship for its name, type, size, speed, course, destination, distance from you and photo links.
  • With location (◎) on, a ship that will pass within 0.5 nm of you in the next 20 minutes turns the button red, vibrates and shows a warning.
  • The number on the button is how many ships are shown.

Good to know

  • Ships send their name, type and size only every few minutes. Until then a ship is grey; once seen, the phone remembers it.
  • Positions can lag from seconds to a few minutes, and coverage depends on volunteer receivers.
  • Many small boats don't send AIS. Keep a lookout; this doesn't replace one.
  • It uses data only while AIS is on and the app is open. Tap AIS again to turn it off.
Overlay PDF maps

Lay a park or trail map PDF over the map, e.g. a brochure from the park's website. Tap PDF on the map, then Add an overlay PDF map… It's saved on this phone and shows offline.

Placing it

  • Outback places it from the PDF's own map data (GeoPDF) or from printed GPS Coordinates labels when the PDF has them. Otherwise it starts over the area you're looking at.
  • Drag the 4 pink points onto the spots they belong, or ✥ to move the whole map. The others follow as you drag one. The PDF slider on the right makes it see-through.
  • Map came in sideways (some brochures are printed that way)? Tap ⟳ 90° until it reads the right way up, then place it.
  • Save keeps it; Cancel drops the change. Later, Adjust in the PDF list brings the points back.

Auto-align

  • For maps that show water (a lake, river or bay): place it roughly first, within a few hundred metres, then tap Auto-align.
  • It finds the light-blue water on the map and fits it to OpenStreetMap's shoreline, turning, shifting and scaling the map a little. The OpenStreetMap shoreline shows as a dashed blue line so you can check it, and the message gives how far off the shoreline was before and after.
  • Not right? Undo align puts the points back. Fine-tune by dragging, then Save.
  • The first time needs a connection, to get the water for the area. It's kept with the map, so later it works offline.
  • Brochure maps are simplified, so small coves can still be off by a little. Maps without water can't be auto-aligned.

Good to know

  • The first PDF import downloads the PDF reader (about 10 MB). Big PDFs can take a while.
  • One page is used: the one with map data or GPS labels, otherwise the first page.
  • Built-in maps (in the PDF list) come already placed: tap Add once while online. Now: Tomales Bay boat-in camping (National Park Service), Del Valle Regional Park (East Bay Regional Park District) Sacramento–San Joaquin Delta boating (California Coastal Commission, 2005: marinas, pump-outs and fuel docks may have changed), and the five SF Bay Area Water Trail maps (North Central, South Central and South Bay, North San Pablo Bay, Suisun Marsh; 2017). The Water Trail maps' tables and zoomed insets sit over parts of the map, at their own scale.
  • Overlay PDF maps you saved in the GPS Map app can be copied in from the PDF list.
  • Overlay PDF maps are included in Back up.
Tomales Bay and San Francisco Bay

Official info for two popular Bay Area paddles. Rules and closures change; check these before you go.

Tomales Bay (Point Reyes National Seashore)

  • Boat-in camping: beaches, permits (Recreation.gov) and rules.
  • Boat-in camping map (PDF): launches, beaches open or closed to camping, toilets.
  • Kayaking around Point Reyes and the low-impact boat camping guide.
  • Overnight parking only at Tomales Bay Resort, Miller Boat Launch and Lawson's Landing. Pelican Point, Duck Island and the east side of Hog Island are closed all year; Blue Gum Beach is closed March 1 – June 30 for seal pupping.

San Francisco Bay

  • San Francisco Bay Area Water Trail: launch sites, access and printable maps.
  • Water Trail web map: every launch and destination with directions and photos.
  • Conditions on the Bay: tides, currents and other hazards.
  • Dolphin Club: boating on the Bay: the central Bay's currents, ship traffic and hazards.
  • For shipping lanes and depths, use the Chart layer on the map (NOAA charts).
Sharing and printing a trip
  • Share plan (Trip tab, under Tracks) writes the trip out as plain text anyone can read: day and start time, route, every waypoint with coordinates, leg, bearing and estimated arrival, your markers and the day's forecast. Copy text to paste it into a club forum or email; Share… sends it with the GPX file where the phone allows; GPX file is for people with a GPS or map app.
  • Print makes a one-page trip sheet: the map with the route and numbered waypoints, the waypoint table, the day's forecast and the tide/current and wind graphs, with the planned start marked. Check the preview, then tap Print, or choose Save as PDF in the print dialog.
  • Get the forecast first so it's on the page, and pick the map layer you want printed (Chart, Street, Topo or Sat) before tapping Print.
  • Turn on 🚻 and P on the map first to have restrooms and parking on the printed map.
  • For a waterproof case: print in colour if you can, fold once, and carry a proper chart or map too.
